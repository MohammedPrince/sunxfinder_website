# SunXFinder: Competitors and the Gap

Researched 2026-11-19 from public sources only. Every row is in [competitors.csv](competitors.csv) with its link. The idea being tested is in [idea.md](idea.md).

## The short answer

- **No direct competitor was found in Sudan.** No public website or app in Sudan lets a customer get and compare offers from several solar companies. The model exists elsewhere in Africa: SolarKwotes and Hohm in South Africa, SolarMarket in Uganda.
- **The real competition is how people buy today:** asking friends and Facebook groups, going to a market shop, calling one installer's page, or keeping the generator.
- **Pieces of SunXFinder exist separately:** one installer publishes a price guide (Shams Alnil), one tech blog has a calculator (Mutajadidah), and classifieds and price lists show parts prices. **Nobody combines sizing, several written offers and ratings.**
- **The biggest documented buyer fear is weak or fake equipment.** The Sudanese standards body itself warns about it.

## What could not be checked

- **Customer reviews of Sudanese installers.** Google Maps and Facebook ratings weren't readable without a login, and no review site covers Sudanese installers. So the complaints below come from news reports and official statements, not individual reviews. Every theme is marked **thin**.
- **Pages that blocked reading:** OpenSooq Sudan (403), ENF Solar directory (403), Alramah Solar (expired certificate), SolaVolt's Facebook page (login wall).
- **Facebook groups and WhatsApp**, probably the biggest "competitor", have no public numbers.

Next step: read 20 to 30 real comments in the launch city's solar Facebook groups and on 3 to 5 installers' pages by hand, and add the themes here with links.

## 2. Price range

### For the customer

Every alternative that helps a buyer decide is **free**: price guides, calculators, classifieds, asking around. SunXFinder must stay free for customers. Nobody pays to compare.

### The system itself (what customers are comparing)

| | Price | What it is | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lowest | **USD ~2,700** | "A system" (size not stated), Feb 2026, down from USD 5,700 before 2023 | [Alzaawia](https://alzaawia.net/114920) |
| Middle | **USD 4,200–5,400** | 5 kW hybrid + 10 kWh lithium, tier-1, installed, Aug 2026 | [Shams Alnil](https://shamsalnil.com/solar-prices-sudan/) |
| Highest | **USD ~5,000** for 10 kW | "Typical household 10 kW system", Khartoum | [AP via ABC News](https://abcnews.com/International/wireStory/sudans-war-driven-blackouts-push-residents-solar-power-136235978) |

The sizes differ, so treat this as "about USD 2,700 to 5,400 for a home system" rather than a strict range. Prices for the same system vary a lot between sellers. That spread is why comparing is worth something.

### For companies (what SunXFinder will later charge)

The only priced comparable is **paid contact lists**: USD 149–299 for a list, or USD 0.20 a lead (Fastbase). Those are cold contacts for businesses. SunXFinder offers warm buyers asking for a quote. `/founder-pricing` should start from what a company earns on one installation, not from these lists.

## 3. Positioning map

Two things matter to a nervous solar buyer: **is the advice neutral?** (does it show several sellers, or sell its own) and **does it help me decide?** (sizing, written offers, trust signals, or just a list).

```
                         HELPS YOU DECIDE
                                ▲
                                │
     Installers (Shams Alnil,   │        ★ SunXFinder target
     Alramah, Solarman,         │          (size + several written
     SolaVolt): site visit,     │           offers + ratings)
     written quote              │
                                │   Mutajadidah calculator
                                │   (sizes, no companies)
   SELLS ITS OWN ───────────────┼───────────────────── NEUTRAL / MANY SELLERS
                                │
     Friend's installer         │   Facebook groups, WhatsApp
     (one opinion)              │   OpenSooq classifieds
                                │   sudan-prices.com
     Market shop                │   ENF / paid lists
                                │
                                ▼
                          JUST A LIST
```

The top-right corner is empty. Neutral sources don't help the buyer decide, and the sources that help the buyer decide are selling their own systems.

## 5. The gap

**In Sudan, nobody neutral helps a buyer decide.**

1. **The demand exists and is growing:** grid failure, long cuts (about 12 h a day in parts of Khartoum, [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.net/ebusiness/2026/7/7/أزمة-الكهرباء-تدفع-السودانيين-إلى)), and customs duties removed in August 2026.
2. **The fear is real and official:** the standards body warns about weak equipment (complaint 1).
3. **Prices for the same system vary widely** (USD ~2,700 to 5,400), so comparing has value.
4. **The pieces exist but are separate:** sizing (Mutajadidah), one seller's price guide (Shams Alnil), many sellers with no trust (classifieds, groups). Nobody joins them up.
5. **The model works nearby:** SolarKwotes, Hohm and SolarMarket Uganda show it in markets with power cuts.

What SunXFinder can own: **size your system, then get written offers from several local companies, with brands, models and warranty in writing, and ratings from finished jobs.** This matches the trust positioning in [marketing.md](marketing.md#2-positioning).

Things the gap does **not** cover, so they shouldn't be promised:

- **Price.** SunXFinder doesn't make systems cheaper. Complaint 2 (cost) can only be eased with "start small, expand later" and, later, finance partners.
- **Testing equipment.** SunXFinder can't verify a panel. It can make the brand and model visible in writing, and point to the SSMO 5960 line.

## 6. The threat: who could copy this fastest

| Who | How | How likely | Defence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mutajadidah** | Already has a calculator and an audience reading Sudanese price articles; add a "get quotes" form | Medium–high | Get the launch city's companies signed up first; companies are the hard part to copy |
| **Shams Alnil and other large installers** | Their price guide already draws buyers; they could add "partner installers" | Medium. A seller is rarely trusted as neutral | Stay neutral: never sell systems, label sponsored placements |
| **OpenSooq** | Large classifieds audience; could add a solar "request quotes" flow | Low–medium. Not their focus | Sudan-specific content, Arabic dialect, the quote-check service |
| **Facebook groups / WhatsApp** | Already where people ask | Already there | Be in the groups helping; give companies a tool they can't get in a group (requests, offers, invoices, reports) |
| **Regional platforms** (SolarKwotes, SolarMarket) | Could expand to Sudan | Low while the war lasts | Local presence, SDG pricing, Port Sudan / Khartoum companies |

The real moat is **company density in one city**, plus **rating history**. Both take time to build. Start now.

## What this changes in the marketing plan

- The positioning holds: trust and comparison is an empty corner on the map.
- Add **"Mutajadidah adds quotes"** to the risks. Watch its site monthly.
- Treat installers like Shams Alnil, Alramah and Solarman as **companies to sign up**, not enemies. Their content shows they already explain prices in public.
- Use the SSMO **5960** line in trust posts: it's official, and it shows SunXFinder is on the buyer's side.
